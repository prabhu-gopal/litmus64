# Trademark policy

Licenses cover code. They do not cover branding, which is why the trademark — not the license — is the
real enforcement tool here.

## Reserved

The name **Litmus64**, the wordmark, and the logo.

## Always the full wordmark: "Litmus64," never "Litmus"

One word, one token, no space. This is enforced in every document, talk, and post from day one,
because "Litmus" is crowded and crowded specifically in testing: **LitmusChaos** (CNCF Incubating
chaos engineering, `litmusctl`), **google/litmus** (LLM evaluation), **Puppet Litmus**, **LitmusKt**
(JetBrains Research — litmus testing for Kotlin/Native concurrency), and **Litmus.com** (an
established commercial email-testing mark).

Bare "litmus" will never be ours, and we do not contest it. We own the phrase, not the word: optimise
for "Litmus64 change receipt," not "litmus."

The CLI is `litmus64`, aliased `lx`. **Never `litmus`** — `litmusctl` already exists, and colliding on
a command name is the one mistake that would genuinely confuse users.

## Permitted freely

- Referring to the project by name, truthfully.
- **"Litmus64-compatible"** — granted to anyone who passes the conformance suite, at no cost and with
  no permission required. This makes the trademark an *adoption* mechanism rather than only a
  defensive one, and it is the point.
- Redistributing unmodified builds under the name.
- Forks. Forks are welcome, and **must rename.**

## Not permitted

- Modified builds using the name.
- Use in domain names, product names, or company names.
- Implying endorsement, affiliation, or certification we have not given.

Questions: open an issue, or `trademark@litmus64.com` once the domain resolves (not yet — ADR-0018). We would rather answer a question than send a notice.
